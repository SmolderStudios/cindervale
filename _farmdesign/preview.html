Cindervale Idle · design review · build 0.9.122.3

Farming, on the fourth attempt

The panel is 1,018px tall — the only skill that pushes the page past the window — and most of that height is chrome. Five identical seed dropdowns, three grey rectangles that say "Locked", and a paginated table where every crop's output is cut off mid-word. Four new directions, all at the real panel width.

What I'm not doing again. Ten concepts were previewed in August and all ten were turned down — field rows · two-column workbench · full stage farm · seed-first · painted plots only · ledger · one field card · master–detail · timeline · crop-art cards — and the one that shipped as 0.9.121.3 was reverted the same night. None of the four below reuses those shapes, and none of them puts a painted backdrop behind the controls. The note in memory is chunky cards and grids with visible buttons, no tiny click-tiles and no long tables, so that is the brief I built to.

02

Four directions

Drawn at the panel's real 1,100px width. Same save in all four: two ready, two growing, one empty, three still locked.
Option A

Patches as activity cards

Farming's cards are the only ones in the game that don't look like the skilling cards — which already work, and which you already like. So give a patch the same anatomy: icon in a well, name, one big number, a bar, one wide button. The seed is chosen once at the top, so no card ever holds a dropdown.

+Nothing new to learn — it reads like Woodcutting.
+Five dropdowns become one seed strip.
+Locked patches keep the card shape and say what unlocks them.
−Still eight cards of similar weight; nothing points at what to do first.

Option B

One big button

The Arena gives you one Engage button and everything else is context. Do the same here: one hero action that always names the single best next move and does it, with the eight patches under it as a status board rather than eight separate controls. Come back, press the big thing, leave.

+The 2×2 bulk block disappears into one control.
+Always says when the next patch ripens — the timer the panel never had.
+Shortest panel of the four by a distance.
−Per-patch control moves to a click on the tile; less direct if you want one specific patch.
−The status tiles are small — closest of the four to the click-tiles you rejected.

Option C

Three beds

Patches sort themselves by what they need. Ready, Growing, Empty — three chunky columns, and each column's header carries the button that acts on everything in it. You never scan eight cards to find the two that matter, because the ones that matter are always in the left-hand column with a green border round them.

+What needs you is always in the same place — no hunting.
+Bulk actions stop being a separate block; they're the column headers.
+Locked patches collapse to one quiet line instead of three dead cards.
−Patches move between columns as they ripen, so "patch 3" isn't in a fixed spot.
−Rows inside the columns are closer to a list than a card grid.

Option D

Grow beds

The chunkiest of the four. Each patch is a tall card and the card itself is the progress bar — soil fills it from the bottom as the crop grows, and the crop is drawn big in the middle. You can read the whole field's state from across the room without a single number, and the numbers are still there when you look.

+Growth is legible at a glance, which is the one thing the panel is for.
+Biggest, most tappable buttons of the four.
+No painted backdrop — the fill is CSS, so it costs nothing and themes correctly.
−Eight of these need two rows; the panel stays fairly tall.
−The crop art has to be good at 74px, which is bigger than the icons were drawn for.

04

On generating art for this

You asked me to try it. Here's the honest answer before I spend a night on it.

The painted farm backdrop is the thing that got the last attempt rejected, and it also cost real weight: that one WebP was ~40KB of base64 inside the HTML. cindervale.html is already 5.87 MB against a measured wrapper budget of about 3.4 MB — past that, a slow connection hits the 6-second fetch abort, silently falls back to cache, and stops receiving updates. So raster art in this panel is expensive twice over.

Everything you see above is SVG and CSS: the crop glyphs, the soil fill in option D, the growth bars. It costs a few hundred bytes each, scales to any size, recolours with the theme, and never blurs.

Where generated art would genuinely pay off is the eighteen crops themselves — but those already have hand-drawn SVGs in ICONS, and the ones above are my quick stand-ins for the mockup. If you want the crops to look better, the move is to redraw those eighteen SVGs bigger and richer, not to replace them with images. Say the word and that's a separate pass.

What I need from you

  1. Pick a direction — or tell me which bits of which. A and D are the same idea at two sizes; B and C are genuinely different bets. If none of these land either, say so plainly and I'll stop guessing and ask you what farming should feel like instead.
  2. The crops grid and the Farmhand collapse ship regardless. Both are strict improvements on what's there and neither depends on which field layout wins.
  3. Should locked patches show a progress bar toward their level? I put one in A and D. It might read as encouragement or as clutter — your call.
  4. Tell me if the one seed strip instead of five dropdowns is wrong. It assumes you almost always sow the whole field with one crop, which is what the Plant All button implies, but you know how it actually gets played.
