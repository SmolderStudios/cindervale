The problem

Cindervale only moves one axis at a time.

Three complaints that look separate and aren't.

01

Fight or skill. Never both.

Every hour in the Arena is an hour your Woodcutting doesn't move. The player is always choosing what not to do.

02

4,000 oak logs.

Material stops mattering the moment you out-level it. It piles up, fills the satchel, and gets deleted.

03

One number to come back to.

The welcome-back box reports offline XP and nothing else. That's the entire retention hook.

So build a second axis — and run it off the first.

Not another thing to click. A layer that produces because of what you're already doing.

How it works

Four nouns. That's the whole system.

Everything else on this page is decoration on this loop.

01 — ORDERS

Seven factions

They post work and they have people. You earn Standing with each.

02 — STANDING

A balance, not a rank

You spend it. Mostly on hiring their people away from them.

03 — RETAINERS

Named people

One trait, one job, a level that rises, an upkeep in gold.

04 — THE HALL

Six wings you upgrade

How many you can hold, and how well they work.

Hired → member → employer → institution. Four different relationships to the same seven NPCs, without a single new zone or skill.
The three ideas

What makes it not a reputation grind.

The sink

Set it once. It never asks again.

Pick an item, an Order, and a keep-floor. From then on everything you produce above that floor is taken as it's produced, paid in gold and Standing.

No hand-in button. No board to visit. An idle game shouldn't ask you to click "deliver 300 cobalt."

  • Satchel stops filling — consigned items never need a slot
  • Oak logs still have a price at level 99
  • Flood one material and its rate saturates, recovering over days — so you rotate what you produce

The parallel axis

Your crew works off your clock.

A retainer's output is a fraction of your current rate — not a wall-clock timer.

Fight in the Arena with two on Assist and they chop and smelt at 25% of your rate for the whole fight. That's the fix for combat and skilling being mutually exclusive.

It can never out-earn active play, because it is a slice of active play.

The ladder

Standing is spent, not stacked.

Standard guild rep tops out and the tab dies. Here Standing is a balance — spent hiring, buying, and calling in favours.

A separate lifetime high-water mark drives your title, so you still get the prestige beats. Two numbers, two jobs, neither goes stale.

  • Because you buy people, and people can always be better people
  • No Order ever reaches "max rank, nothing left here"
  • Poaching costs Standing with every other Order — that's the whole rivalry system, and it's recoverable

The roster

Seven Orders, sorted by what they want.

Not by skill cluster. A "mining guild" goes dead the second you switch to combat, and half your roster sleeps. Sorted this way, all seven stay live no matter what you're training.

Seven sinks — and only one deletes gold.

A sink that just burns money reads as a tax. A sink that trades money for tempo reads as a decision.

The Charter fee

The terminal sink. One enormous, voluntary, permanent purchase at the top of the curve.

flat

Crests & banners

Pure vanity, zero balance risk, and players reliably buy it.

—

Not a sink

Nothing here gates content. Gold only ever buys it sooner.

The rule I'd hold hardest: the moment gold is required to reach something rather than reach it sooner, the economy becomes a soft paywall on your own game — and Cindervale has three character slots, so that mistake gets made three times.
Build order

Phase 1 ships on its own.

It solves the material-overflow problem by itself, and nothing above it is a prerequisite. If it lands, the rest gets easy to justify.

PHASE 1

Consignments + 2 Orders

Standing, titles, the auto-sink. No retainers, no Hall. Complete and shippable alone.

medium
PHASE 2

The Hall + retainers

The Roll, the assignment board, Assist / Gather / Craft / Idle. Where it proves it's fun.

large
PHASE 3

Four more Orders

Hall wings, recruiting draw. Mostly data against render functions that exist.

medium
PHASE 4

Signature mechanics

Guard, Study, the Muster, the Float, the Offering. Independent — one per release.

per feature
PHASE 5

The Charter

Endgame, crest builder, the Quiet Market.

medium

Save surface is the real cost

Retainers are an array of objects with levels and jobs — the largest new state shape the game has taken. Every field needs a normalizeState guard and an _audit_tests.js assertion, because none of it throws when it breaks.

Assist touches every skill

It's a multiplier on every rate at once. It wants to be one function reading mods(), not a per-skill special case — or it drifts out of sync exactly like the tooltip numbers did before the audit.

Grading is its own release

It touches every crafted item — tooltip, satchel, presets, sockets, enchants, sell path, compendium. Ship the Assay without it first. Add grading only if the Orders land.

The honest version

Cindervale has plenty to do and very little to decide.

The case for

A second axis that only pays for commitment. A permanent automatic sink for the mountains of material the game already produces. A parallel ladder that keeps ticking through the skill-tree dead zones. And a doorway for four features currently parked on the horizon with no path into a player's hands.

The case against

It's the biggest thing you'd have built since combat. But Phase 1 is small and self-contained and answers the overflow problem on its own — if it lands, everything above it gets easier to justify. If it doesn't, you've lost one release, not a quarter.
