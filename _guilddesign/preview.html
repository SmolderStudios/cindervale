The whole thing, in four steps.

If you only read one part of this page, read this. Everything else is detail.

STEP 1

Join a guild

There are seven. Each one buys different stuff and does a different favour for you.

STEP 2

Earn reputation

Mostly by selling them materials automatically — you set it up once and forget it.

STEP 3

Hire their workers

Reputation is money you can only spend at that guild. Mostly you spend it on people.

STEP 4

Workers do jobs

They gather, craft, guard you in fights, or copy your work into a second skill.

The short version: you get a second team that produces stuff at the same time as you, paid for with the junk you were throwing away anyway.
The words

Every new term, in plain English.

These are the actual labels I'd put in the game — not fantasy names. If a player has to guess what a tab does, the tab is broken.

Guild
A group in the city you can join. Seven of them. Not a group of other players — Cindervale is single-player, so these are NPC organisations.
Reputation
Points you earn with one specific guild. Works like gold, but you can only spend it at that guild. It goes down when you spend it.
Rank
Your title at a guild — Porter, Foreman, Master, and so on. Based on total reputation you've ever earned, so it never goes down when you spend. Rank unlocks what you're allowed to buy; reputation pays for it.
Auto-Sell
You pick an item, pick a guild, and say "keep 200 for me, sell the rest." From then on every extra one you make is sold automatically. No clicking, no delivery screen.
Worker
An NPC you hired. Has a name, a level, one special quality, a wage in gold per hour, and one job.
Guild Hall
Your building. Six rooms you upgrade. Decides how many workers you can have and how well they work.
Wages
What your workers cost you, in gold per hour. A worker set to Resting costs nothing.
Helper (a job)
The important one. A Helper copies part of whatever you're doing right now into a different skill. Mining? Your Helper smiths at 25% of your speed at the same time.
Bounty Zone
One combat zone each week pays double or triple reputation. The Warriors' Guild picks it. It usually picks a low-level zone.
Rule for the whole feature: the label says what it does. Flavour goes in the description underneath, never in the button. "Workers" is a tab; "the Roll" is a riddle.
Why bother

Cindervale only moves one thing at a time.

Three complaints that look separate and aren't.

01

Fight or skill. Never both.

Every hour in the Arena is an hour your Woodcutting doesn't move. The player is always choosing what not to do.

02

4,000 oak logs.

Materials stop mattering the moment you out-level them. They pile up, fill the satchel, and get thrown away.

03

One number to come back to.

The welcome-back box reports offline XP and nothing else. That's the whole reason to reopen the game.

Guilds fix all three with one system.

A second team that produces alongside you, funded by the materials you were already throwing away, and it has news for you every time you come back.

The three ideas

What makes this not a reputation grind.

Idea 1 — Auto-Sell

Set it once. It never asks you again.

Pick an item. Pick a guild. Say how many to keep for yourself. Done.

From then on, every extra one you produce is sold the moment you make it. You get gold and reputation. There is no delivery screen and no button to press.

  • Your satchel stops filling up — sold items never take a slot
  • Oak logs are still worth something at level 99
  • Sell too many of one thing and its price drops, recovering over a few days — so you rotate what you make
For example You set oak logs → Suppliers' Guild, keep 200. You go chop for three hours. You come back with 200 oak logs in the bag and +34,000 gold and +1,100 reputation you never had to click for.

Idea 2 — Helpers

A worker can copy what you're doing into another skill.

This is the one that matters. A worker set to Helper picks a skill, then produces in that skill at a percentage of your own current speed.

So combat and skilling stop being a choice. You fight; your helpers smith and cook off the back of it.

For example You're fighting in the Arena. Bram is a Helper set to Smithing. He smiths at 26% of the speed you're currently fighting at — so an hour of combat also gets you an hour of 26%-speed Smithing.

It can never beat playing actively, because it's always a slice of playing actively.

Idea 3 — Reputation is money

You spend reputation. It goes down.

Most games make guild rep a bar that fills to 100% and then the guild is finished with you forever.

Here reputation is a balance you spend — mostly on hiring workers away from that guild. Your rank is tracked separately from total earned, so your title never drops when you spend.

  • You're buying people, and there's always a better person
  • No guild ever hits "max rank, nothing left here"
  • Hiring someone away annoys the other guilds a little — that's the whole rivalry system, and it wears off

The roster

Seven guilds, named for what they do.

Sorted by what they buy, not by which skill they belong to. A "mining guild" goes dead the second you switch to combat — this way all seven stay useful no matter what you're training.

Suppliers' Guild

BUYS IN BULK

Buys food, logs, potions, fish and seeds. Any amount, forever.

Only hereThe best auto-sell prices in the game. This is where most of your leftover materials go.

Craftsmen's Guild

BUYS QUALITY

Wants one excellent item, not a thousand rough ones. Pays huge for a good one.

Only hereItem quality. Everything you craft secretly rolls Crude / Common / Fine / Masterwork. They pay for the good rolls, and a Fine item gets a small stat bonus.

Warriors' Guild

PAYS FOR KILLS

Pays you to fight — but only in the zone they name this week.

Only hereBodyguards, who take a share of the damage in fights instead of you. And the bounty zone, which usually picks a low-level zone, so old content is worth going back to.

Merchants' Guild

BANKING

Deposit gold and get more back a day later. Also sells big stacks for you without crashing the price.

Only hereDeposits. Put in 240,000g, get 255,600g back in 18 hours. The only thing in the game that makes having gold interesting.

Scholars' Guild

PAYS IN UNLOCKS

Doesn't pay gold. Pays in recipes, blueprints and revealed drop tables.

Only hereResearch. This is how the stuff on your roadmap actually reaches players — T6 tools, Ember Dust recipes, combat-tier crafting. Each becomes a project with a progress bar.

The Temple

TAKES ANYTHING

Accepts literally any item in the game. Gives you a timed buff instead of gold.

Only hereAn "offer all junk" button that empties every worthless stack in your bag at once and turns it into a real buff. Nothing is ever worth zero again.

Thieves' Guild

HIDDEN · GAMBLING

You don't join it. It finds you, off a rare drop.

Only hereJobs you can actually fail. Bet reputation, double or nothing. The only real downside risk in the game — which is exactly why it's optional and hidden.

Your own guild

ENDGAME

Once your hall is big enough, you register your own guild for 12,000,000 gold.

Only hereThe direction reverses. You post the jobs, the other six guilds send workers to do them, and they pay you.

Six is enough to ship. The Thieves' Guild is a bolt-on — build it last, or never.

On the names: plain names are the labels. Each guild can still have a fantasy name in its description if you want the flavour — "the Suppliers' Guild, known as the Long Table" — but the tab, the button and the tooltip say Suppliers' Guild, so nobody has to work out what a Long Table is.

Gold sinks

Seven ways to spend gold — and only one destroys it.

A sink that just deletes your money feels like a tax. A sink that trades money for speed feels like a choice.

per worker / hr

Wages

The main one. Scales with how many workers you chose to hire — and Resting costs nothing, so you can never over-hire yourself into trouble.

45k–400k

Reshuffle who's for hire

Gold buys you a new set of three candidates. It never buys a guaranteed good one.

up to ⅓ of your gold

Bank deposits

Parks your gold instead of destroying it. The cost is not having it for a day.

180,000g

Change the bounty zone early

Buys you time. Limited to once a week so it can't be spammed.

gold + reputation

Hall upgrades

Costs both, so no amount of gold on its own finishes your hall.

12,000,000g

Register your own guild

The big one. A single enormous, optional, permanent purchase for people at the top.

flat price

Crests and banners

Pure decoration, no balance impact, and players buy it reliably.

—

What none of them do

None of these lock content behind gold. Gold only ever gets you there sooner.

The rule I'd hold hardest: the moment gold is required to reach something instead of just reaching it faster, your own game turns into a soft paywall — and Cindervale has three character slots, so you'd make that mistake three times over.
Build order

Phase 1 is worth shipping on its own.

It fixes the leftover-materials problem by itself, and nothing after it is required. If players like it, the rest is easy to justify.

PHASE 1

Auto-Sell + 2 guilds

Reputation, ranks, and the auto-sell system. No workers, no hall yet. Complete on its own.

medium
PHASE 2

The hall + workers

Hiring, the worker list, the jobs screen. Helper / Gatherer / Crafter / Resting only. This is where it proves it's fun.

large
PHASE 3

Four more guilds

Plus hall upgrades. Mostly new data plugged into screens that already exist.

medium
PHASE 4

The special stuff

Bodyguards, research, the bounty zone, deposits, the Temple. All independent — ship one per update.

one at a time
PHASE 5

Your own guild

Endgame, crest builder, Thieves' Guild.

medium

Save files are the real cost

Workers are a list of objects with levels and jobs — the biggest new thing the save file has ever had to hold. Every field needs a normalizeState guard and an _audit_tests.js check, because none of it throws an error when it breaks.

Helpers touch every skill at once

A Helper multiplies rates across the whole game. It needs to be one function reading mods(), not a special case per skill — otherwise it drifts out of sync exactly like the tooltip numbers did before the audit.

Item quality is its own update

Grading touches every crafted item — tooltips, satchel, presets, sockets, enchants, selling, compendium. Ship the Craftsmen's Guild without it first and add quality later, only if guilds land well.

The honest version

Cindervale has plenty to do and very little to decide.

Worth building

It gives players a second thing producing at the same time as them, a permanent home for the mountain of materials the game already makes, a progress track that keeps moving through the dead spots in the skill trees, and a way for four features already on your roadmap to actually reach a player's hands.

The catch

It's the biggest thing you'd have built since combat. But phase 1 is small and self-contained and fixes the materials problem on its own — if it works, everything after it is easy to justify. If it doesn't, you've spent one update, not a quarter.
