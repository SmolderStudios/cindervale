Cindervale Idle · design proposal · build 0.9.122.30

Thieving is stalls, and stalls give things

Gold alone is not a reason for a skill. What makes Thieving worth building is that a stall sells something — so stealing from it gives you that thing. A seed stall gives seeds. A gem stall gives gems, sometimes. That is a supply line no other skill provides, and it is the answer to "what does this bring besides money".

It also has a home already: the Nightmarket, one of the ten guilds, is literally called Thieves and currently gates on Agility because Agility is the closest thing the game has.

00

What the Nightmarket is

Since you asked — it is one of the ten guilds, and it has been sitting there unused.
A guild
One of ten. You join it, and it posts a daily quest board. Small, standard and long quests that reset once a calendar day. Completing them pays reputation, guild tokens and a little XP in the skill it names.
Reputation
Six ranks: Runner, Lookout, Fence, Broker, Shadowbroker, Master of the Nightmarket. 1,500 rep for the second, 150,000 for the last. Each rank turns on a passive perk permanently.
Its perks
+4% / +8% / +14% gold from every sale, +4% Agility speed, +6% Agility XP. A guild of thieves whose rewards buff running laps, because there is no skill for it to buff instead.
The unused flag
It carries fence:true and nothing in the game reads it. Somebody meant for this guild to buy stolen goods. Nothing was ever built to sell it any.
01

What it brings besides gold

This is the whole argument for the skill.
Pillar 1

A stall gives what it sells

Coins every time. And a chance at whatever that stall trades in — seeds, fish, herbs, ore, gems, silk, logs.

So Thieving is the one skill that supplies other skills. Not fast, and never reliably, but across all of them at once from a single level ladder.

Pillar 2

It is the way in

Want to try Farming with no seeds? Steal seeds. Want to cut gems with Mining at 20? Steal gems. Every skill in Cindervale currently demands you grind its own inputs before you can play it.

Thieving is the shortcut past that first wall — a worse rate than doing the skill properly, but you can start.

Pillar 3

It can fail

Every one of the twelve skills always succeeds. Start it, the bar fills, you get the thing. Nothing in the game can simply not work.

A steal has a success chance. Fail and you lose the attempt and get caught for a few seconds. That is a verb the game does not have, and it is what the passive tree buys instead of speed.

03

The guardrails

A skill that hands out every other skill's materials can wreck all of them. Four rules stop it.
Rule 1
A stall never beats the skill it copies. Target roughly a third of what dedicated gathering pays per hour. Thieving buys you breadth — one ladder feeding seven skills — and pays for it in depth.
Rule 2
It gives the tier BELOW what you could gather. The fish stall at Thieving 60 hands you what a Fishing 40 player catches. It gets you started and it never replaces the ladder.
Rule 3
You cannot pick what you get. The stall decides. Wanting one specific material is exactly when thieving is worst, and that is deliberate — it is a trickle of everything, not a tap for the thing you need.
Rule 4
The gem stall stays rare. Gems gate the whole Jeweler and enchant economy. 15% on the highest stall is a treat, not a supply. This is the number to watch hardest in testing.
Stolen goods, and the fence that already exists.

Pickpocketing people gives stolen goods — six to eight new items (a silver plate, a signet, a sealed purse) carrying a hot flag. The general store pays a third for them. The Nightmarket pays full price.

That is fence:true finally reading, it gives the guild a reason to exist beyond its quest board, and it costs one term in effectiveItemSell rather than a second economy.

04

Failing, without breaking idle

The one thing that could make this skill unshippable.
Cindervale's promise is that you set an activity and walk away.

A skill whose action fails half the time reads as broken while idling — the same shape as the fire going out in Cooking, which took a player report to find. So:

Failure never stops the activity. It costs the attempt and a few seconds of being caught, then it carries on. XP pays on the attempt, not the success, so a thief idling at 45% still levels — slower, and with far less loot. Success rate is a throughput dial, never a wall.

And the tree buys success rather than speed, which makes it the first tree in the game where a point changes what you can do rather than how fast you do it.

The skill, the success roll, being caught, XP on the attempt — and the eight stalls with their loot tables. This is the whole pitch: a skill that gives you other skills' materials. Shippable and interesting with nothing else.

07

What I need you to decide

Everything above is reversible except these.
How hard do the stalls undercut gathering? A third of dedicated rate is my starting number. Too generous and Farming, Foraging and Fishing all become optional; too stingy and nobody uses the skill for anything but gold. My call: a third, one tier below, and no choosing what you get. Tune from there.
Does failure cost anything beyond time? Being caught for a few seconds is a throughput cost and nothing else. Damage on the guard and noble acts would make food a thieving expense — and would mean an idling thief can die. My call: time only on stalls, small damage on the two hardest people. Never enough to kill from full.
Does the Nightmarket re-gate to Thieving? It is right, and it is a change to a live guild: anyone who joined at Agility 15 needs grandfathering, and its perks currently buff Agility. My call: re-gate, grandfather existing members, repoint the perks. The guild is called Thieves.
Can you steal seeds you have not unlocked? The seed stall is the most interesting act in the list precisely because seeds are slow to come by. Handing out seeds above your Farming level would let Thieving skip the farming ladder outright. My call: capped at your own Farming tier. The stall gets you volume, never access.
Thieving or Ranged first? Ranged is the bigger hole — a SOON pill in the combat panel with half its plumbing already built. Thieving is smaller and better timed: the shop just got 32 things to buy, and this is a skill about acquiring things. My call: Thieving P1 first. One phase proves whether the stall loop is fun before anything else gets built on it.
