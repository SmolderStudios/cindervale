Guilds — eight factions, one tab

Cindervale is single-player, so a "guild" here is not a player group — it's an NPC faction that wants something from you and pays in a currency gold can't buy. Each guild owns a slice of the game that currently has no long-term sink: leftover materials, redundant drops, capped skills, the dead zone between tree tiers. You join, you rank up, you unlock perks and a private shop. Below: placement options, the hall, a full guild page, and every mechanic a guild could plug into — with sigils left as ART TBD placeholders.

Stock rules that keep it honest
  • No raw combat stats. Guild gear is skilling gear, charms, and consumables — never a sword.

The Patron slot

one active guild — a real choice you re-make weekly

You can join all eight. But only one is your Patron, and the Patron buff is big enough to actually shape what you do that session. Swapping is free but has a 6h cooldown — long enough to be a decision, short enough to never feel punishing.

Standing & rivalries

the risky one — two versions, soft and hard
Soft version ship this
PairTensionEffect
Deepdelve ↔ HollowrootStrip-mining vs. the groveRank-up in one gives −1 rank-worth of favor to the other. Recoverable.
Emberwright ↔ Gilded LedgerPrice-fixing vs. free marketOptional contracts that pay extra to spite the other.
Iron Covenant ↔ Grey VigilArmy vs. specialistsPurely flavour text. No mechanical cost.
Athenaeum ↔ NightfallPublish vs. hoardNightfall contracts are hidden until Athenaeum standing drops.
Everything is recoverable by grinding. Nothing is ever permanently closed.
Hard version don't
  • Joining Deepdelve permanently locks Hollowroot for that character.
  • Creates real build identity and a genuine reason to roll a second character.
  • But: Cindervale has 3 character slots and a huge time investment per character. Locking half the content behind a choice made at Mining 20 is how you get a player who quits at hour 80.
  • And it doubles the testing surface for every contract, perk and shop item.
Recommendation: soft only. The fantasy of "I chose a side" survives fine with a favor penalty; the frustration of an unreachable Grandmaster title does not.

Guild expeditions

idle-inside-the-idle — the sailing pattern, generalised

Sailing already proved this shape works: dispatch, wait, collect. Guilds can reuse the exact same loop for land. Send guild members out; they come back with things you can't farm directly.

Why this earns its place
  • It's the only way to progress while doing a different skill. Currently combat and skilling are mutually exclusive; an expedition running in the background is combat-flavoured progress during a woodcutting session.
  • It gives voidheart a second faucet that isn't "kill the same boss 200 times".
  • Sworn are a resource you recruit with favor — a soft cap on how much you can parallelise.
  • Reuses the sailing timer/dispatch code almost wholesale.
Watch out: if expeditions out-earn active play, the game plays itself. Cap expedition yield at roughly 35% of active rate and make the good stuff materials you can't otherwise get, not raw XP.

What each guild uniquely contributes

no two guilds should feel like a reskin

The failure mode is eight identical "deliver N, get perk" tracks. Each one needs a signature mechanic that only it has.

GuildSignature mechanicWhat it means at the table
Emberwright CompactCommissions The only guild that asks for finished goods, not raw materials. Forces you to actually use Smithing/Crafting output instead of selling bars. Pays in recipes.
Hollowroot CircleRestraint Contracts that ask you to not do things — leave a patch fallow, skip a tree tier for a day. Cheap to build, memorable, and the only guild with a mechanic that isn't "more".
Deepdelve UnionClaims Stake veins that produce ore offline. Turns Mining from the game's biggest time sink into something with a passive floor.
Iron CovenantWar table A rotating focus zone with big bonuses. Redirects players into content they've out-levelled and forgotten.
Grey VigilBestiary bounties Pays for Monster Log completion, not kills. Makes the collection log a progression system instead of a scrapbook. Natural home for pet lures and rare-spawn tells.
Saltborn CompanyStanding routes Set a repeating voyage that fires without you re-dispatching. Fixes the "sailing needs babysitting" tax.
Ashen AthenaeumResearch Spend materials to run a timer that unlocks knowledge — recipes, drop-table reveals, enchant tiers. The only guild whose reward is information.
Gilded LedgerThe market Prices drift weekly. Buy low on materials, sell high on gear. A pure gold sink with a reason to check in that isn't combat or skilling.

Scope — what I'd build, and in what order

and what I'd cut outright
Ship order
PhaseContentsWeight
1Guild tab + hall + 3 guilds (Deepdelve, Emberwright, Iron Covenant) + standing contracts + favor + 7 ranks + perks. Playable, complete, shippable alone.large

Cut list
  • Player-to-player guilds. No server, no cohort, no.
  • Hard faction locks. Soft penalties only — see §10.
  • A visual Lodge/map. Six upgrade cards do the same job for a fraction of the work and zero art debt.
  • Guild-specific combat gear. Muddies the gear-tier ladder that was just balanced. Charms and skilling gear only.
  • Favor decay. Punishing an idle-game player for idling is self-defeating.
  • The Wayfarer's Table. Agility doesn't have enough surface to carry a guild. Fold its ideas into Saltborn.
The honest case for guilds: Cindervale's late game is well-supplied with things to do and thin on reasons to choose between them. Guilds add a second currency axis that only rewards commitment, a sink for the mountains of material the game already produces, and a home for at least five features currently parked on the horizon with no path to a player's hands.

The honest risk: it's a big surface. Eight guilds × seven ranks × four contract shapes is a lot of state to guard in normalizeState, and every one of those needs an _audit_tests.js assertion or it'll silently break a save. Phase 1 with three guilds is the version that proves it's fun before that bill comes due.
