Alchemy shows recipes, not potions

Thirty-four brewing recipes make twenty distinct potions, and ten of those potions have more than one recipe — so 24 of the 34 cards are duplicates of each other. Wisdom Potion III alone occupies four separate cards. And not one card anywhere on the panel says what the potion it makes actually does.

01
The panel is organised around the recipe, and you shop for the potion. Wanting an Ironhide II means finding and comparing two cards that sit in different rows, one called "Brew Ironhide Potion II" and one called "Temper Ironhide II (dust)".
02
Nothing says what any of it is for. The hero number is xp/hr. Whether Swiftness III is worth brewing over Wisdom III depends entirely on effects the panel never prints.
03
Two kinds of card share one grid. Tiered families carry a <select> in the card head; singletons don't. Same size, different anatomy, three of them on the skilling tab and four on combat.
04
Level-locked and out-of-ingredients look identical. Both are dimmed. "Unlocks at Lv 88" and "you need three more Witchhazel" are very different problems with the same visual answer.
05
Titles run out of room. "Brew Philosopher's…", "Brew Healing…", and "Brew Warrior's Triple Brew" wraps to three lines.
A bug I found while counting. ALCH_FAMILY and ALCH_TIER_LABEL both map al4, al5 and al6 to tier II of Swiftness, Bountiful and Wisdom — but those three recipes do not exist. Nothing crashes, because the dropdown is built from the real act list, so the phantom tiers are silently skipped. It does mean every one of those families reads I → III with no II, and carries two dead entries in a lookup table. Was tier II cut on purpose, or lost?

Four directions

At the panel's real 1,110px. Real potions, real effects, real ingredients.
Option A

One card per potion

The card stops being a recipe and becomes the potion. Its effect is the line you read first, the tier is pips rather than a dropdown, and the alternate recipes collapse into a "2 ways to make this" row inside the card you were already looking at. Thirty-four cards become twenty.

+Kills all 24 duplicate cards outright.
+Every card finally says what it makes and what that does.
+One card anatomy — no more select-or-not.
−The monster-part recipes lose their own names, which some of them are fond of.

Option B

Grouped by what it does

You do not open alchemy wanting "a recipe" — you open it wanting more speed, or more XP, or something that heals for 450. So the panel is sectioned by outcome, each section a row of the potions that produce it, and the tier ladder reads left to right inside its own group.

+Answers the question you actually arrived with.
+The tier ladder is visible as a ladder — and the missing II is obvious.
+Replaces the two big tab buttons: the groups are the navigation.
−Transmutation has no "effect", so it needs a group of its own that breaks the rule.
−Cards get smaller to fit six groups; ingredients move to hover.

Option C

The cabinet

Leads with what you already have, because the real question in alchemy is never "what can I make" — it's "have I got enough Swiftness for tonight". Stock is the hero number, the effect sits under it, and Brew is a top-up. Low stock sorts to the front.

+Matches how the skill is actually played: you stockpile, then idle.
+"You are out of Wisdom III" is visible without hunting.
−Buries the xp/hr, which is what you want while levelling the skill.
−Sorting by stock means cards move; you cannot learn where things are.

Option D

Three at a time

Fewer, much bigger. Three tiles across instead of four, each showing the potion at size, its effect as the headline, and every recipe that makes it as rows you pick between — so the four ways to make Wisdom III are a choice inside one tile rather than four cards scattered across the grid.

+The alternate recipes are a comparison, which is what they are for.
+Biggest, clearest cards of the four; the potion art finally has room.
−Twenty potions at three across is seven rows — the tallest panel here by far.
−Needs a filter or a tab to stay usable, which reintroduces navigation.

What I need from you

  1. Pick a direction. A is the structural fix and the smallest change. B reorganises the whole panel around outcomes. C is a different idea about what alchemy is for. D is A with the recipe comparison made explicit, at the cost of height.
  2. The duplicate collapse ships whichever wins — 24 of 34 cards being duplicates is the actual problem, and every option above fixes it. Unless you want the monster-part brews kept as distinct named recipes, in which case say so, because that is the thing collapsing them costs.
  3. Swiftness / Bountiful / Wisdom tier II. The lookup tables think they exist. Should I add the three recipes, or strip the dead entries?
  4. Is xp/hr still the number you want on an alchemy card? Every option demotes it below the potion's effect. That is right for using the skill and arguably wrong for levelling it.
